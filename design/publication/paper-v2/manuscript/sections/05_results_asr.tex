Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 has the lowest \wer{} point estimate in this condition. However, its
interval overlaps those of several competing checkpoints, including \texttt{small.en} and
\texttt{large-v3}; this result should therefore be read as a measured point-estimate ordering
rather than proof of universal statistical superiority.

The speed comparison shows a different ordering. Moonshine/tiny and Moonshine/base are the
fastest measured configurations on this host, while the larger Whisper checkpoints require
substantially more compute. Thus the accuracy and latency rankings are not interchangeable.

The harder \texttt{test-other} condition changes the magnitude of the trade-offs
(Table~\ref{tab:hard-asr}).

Every measured configuration degrades on the harder split, but the degradation is not uniform.
Parakeet shows the smallest measured multiplier in this comparison, while several configurations
approximately double or more than double their \wer{}.

The central result is not that one recognizer is universally preferable. Rather, a clean-speech
measurement hides meaningful differences in robustness. For an interactive system, model
selection should therefore consider the expected acoustic condition rather than relying on a
clean benchmark alone.
