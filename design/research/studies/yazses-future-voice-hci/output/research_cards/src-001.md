## Source Summary

This 2025 survey paper (published in Frontiers of Computer Science / Springer) reviews multimodal natural interaction techniques for Extended Reality headsets covering literature from 2022–2024. The authors systematically classify interaction studies by modality (gesture, gaze, speech, touch, and combinations) and application domain. A key empirical finding is that speech-only interaction studies increased notably in 2024, driven by LLM integration, and that gaze+speech is the most studied two-modality combination (12 studies) in the XR context. The paper identifies locomotion, viewport control, typing, and querying as the interaction tasks with highest growth in research attention during 2024.

## Key Claims

- [EVIDENCE src-001] In the active interaction literature surveyed (2022–2024), modality counts are: Gesture (24 studies), Gaze (13), Speech (7), with combinations Gaze+Speech (12), Gaze+Gesture (4), Gesture+Speech (4), Gaze+Gesture+Speech (2).
- [EVIDENCE src-001] There was a notable increase in speech-related studies in 2024, explicitly attributed to LLM advances enabling more natural conversational interaction.
- [EVIDENCE src-001] Spatial computing platforms position voice, eye-tracking, and hand gestures as the three primary native input modalities, replacing traditional keyboard/mouse.
- [EVIDENCE src-001] Typing and querying tasks in XR saw significant growth in 2024 research, suggesting voice-as-keyboard is an active unsolved problem in spatial computing.
- [HYPOTHESIS] The dominance of gaze+speech combinations (12 studies vs. 4 for gaze+gesture) suggests that voice is the preferred complement to gaze for selection and command disambiguation in XR, which has direct implications for a voice daemon that could read OS-level gaze focus.
- [EVIDENCE src-001] Locomotion and Viewport tasks also grew significantly in 2024, suggesting voice navigation of 3D environments is gaining research momentum.
- [HYPOTHESIS] The small number of three-modality studies (gaze+gesture+speech: 2) indicates this combination is under-explored despite being the most natural human interaction pattern — a gap for YazSes to target.

## Technical Mechanisms

### Architecture / System Design

[EVIDENCE src-001] The paper reviews XR interaction systems that typically pipeline: (1) sensor fusion layer — combining eye-tracker (70–120 Hz), hand-tracking cameras, microphone arrays; (2) intent classification layer — matching raw sensor streams to application commands; (3) feedback rendering layer — visual, haptic, or audio confirmation. Speech-only pipelines in this context add an ASR front-end feeding into either keyword-match or LLM-based intent resolution.

### Core Algorithms or Methods

[EVIDENCE src-001] Speech interaction in XR surveyed systems falls into three categories: keyword/grammar FSM (low latency, low coverage), end-to-end ASR + rule matcher (medium latency), and LLM-mediated natural language command (high coverage, variable latency). [HYPOTHESIS] The trend toward LLM-mediated approaches in 2024 implies that the community is accepting higher latency in exchange for natural language flexibility — a trade-off YazSes must explicitly manage for coding and gaming contexts where sub-200 ms response matters.

### Key Data Structures or APIs

[EVIDENCE src-001] Spatial computing platforms (Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest 3, Microsoft HoloLens) expose standardized gaze and hand tracking APIs that third-party applications can read. [HYPOTHESIS] On Linux desktop (X11/Wayland), equivalent context is available via accessibility tree APIs (AT-SPI2) and active window/cursor position — giving YazSes a viable proxy for "gaze context" without requiring eye-tracking hardware.

## Identified Gaps

### Stated Limitations

- [EVIDENCE src-001] The survey covers only headset-based XR; desktop, phone, and wearable form factors are excluded.
- [EVIDENCE src-001] The survey does not evaluate end-to-end latency of the reviewed systems; latency benchmarks are absent from most surveyed papers.

### Inferred Gaps

- [HYPOTHESIS] No surveyed system addresses offline-first or privacy-preserving voice interaction in XR — all LLM-mediated systems appear to use cloud APIs. This is a clear gap for an offline daemon like YazSes.
- [HYPOTHESIS] The keyboard-replacement use case in XR (dictation for typing tasks) is identified as growing but unsolved — existing solutions don't integrate code-aware context or editor-state awareness.

### Evaluation / Coverage Gaps

- [EVIDENCE src-001] Most surveyed studies report only task completion rate and subjective preference; error recovery workflows and multi-turn disambiguation are rarely evaluated.

## Applicability to Project

[EVIDENCE src-001] The gaze+speech interaction pattern (most studied two-modality combination in XR) directly motivates a YazSes "spatial mode" where the daemon reads the OS accessibility tree to determine what UI element or text field is currently focused, using that context to bias the LLM intent classifier. [HYPOTHESIS] This would enable YazSes to behave like an XR voice interface on a standard Linux/macOS desktop, without requiring eye-tracking hardware — the active window and cursor position serve as a gaze proxy.

[EVIDENCE src-001] The growth of voice-for-typing tasks in XR research confirms that code-aware dictation (understanding variable names, function signatures, boilerplate patterns) is an unsolved problem even in the most advanced spatial computing platforms. YazSes, with its grammar classifier and per-editor profiles, is architecturally closer to solving this than any XR-native voice system surveyed. [HYPOTHESIS] Adding screen-context injection (current file type, IDE language server state) to the YazSes transcription prompt would likely reduce code-dictation error rate substantially.
