## Source Summary

VoiceBench (arXiv October 2024, published in TACL) is the first benchmark designed to evaluate LLM-based voice assistants holistically. Prior work evaluated either ASR accuracy on clean speech or LLM quality on text; VoiceBench bridges the gap by testing the full speech-in/text-out pipeline. It comprises 6,783 spoken instructions covering general knowledge, instruction-following, and safety compliance, with systematic variation in speaker accents, environmental conditions (reverberation, noise), and content complexity (mispronunciations, disfluencies). The paper benchmarks GPT-4o, Gemini, and several open-source voice LLMs, revealing that state-of-the-art models degrade significantly on accented or disfluent speech.

---

## Key Claims

- [EVIDENCE src-002] VoiceBench contains 6,783 synthetic and real spoken instructions across 8 distinct tasks evaluating 3 capability areas: general knowledge, instruction-following, and safety compliance.
- [EVIDENCE src-002] Current LLM-based voice assistants (including GPT-4o) exhibit significant performance degradation on accented speech and in reverberant environments relative to clean-speech baselines.
- [EVIDENCE src-002] Instruction-following capability (not just transcription accuracy) is a distinct failure mode: the model may transcribe correctly but still fail to execute the intended instruction.
- [EVIDENCE src-002] Safety compliance in voice mode is weaker than in text mode — voice-based jailbreak attempts have higher success rates across all tested models.
- [HYPOTHESIS] For an offline daemon like YazSes targeting coding and gaming contexts, instruction-following failure (not transcription failure) will be the dominant quality issue once LLM routing is added — the benchmark's task decomposition validates this hypothesis.
- [EVIDENCE src-002] The benchmark was motivated by the observation that no prior evaluation captured diverse real-world speaker variation, making all published voice assistant quality claims uninterpretable across populations.

## Technical Mechanisms

### Architecture / System Design

[EVIDENCE src-002] VoiceBench tests two system architectures: (1) cascade systems — separate ASR model feeding a text LLM; and (2) end-to-end speech LLMs — models processing audio tokens directly. The cascade approach is more common among open-source systems; end-to-end is GPT-4o's approach. [HYPOTHESIS] For offline deployment, cascade (faster-whisper + local SLM) will remain the practical architecture for 3–5 years given the compute requirements of end-to-end speech LLMs.

### Core Algorithms or Methods

[EVIDENCE src-002] Evaluation methodology uses LLM-as-judge for open-ended tasks and exact-match for factual tasks, with human validation on a subset. Environmental conditions are applied via audio augmentation (room impulse response convolution, additive noise from MUSAN). Speaker variation is introduced through both real accent recordings and TTS synthesis with accent conditioning.

### Key Data Structures or APIs

[EVIDENCE src-002] The benchmark releases code and data at https://github.com/MatthewCYM/VoiceBench. Task categories include: OpenQA, slot-filling, instruction-following (multi-step), safety (adversarial), and domain-specific (coding, medical).

## Identified Gaps

### Stated Limitations

- [EVIDENCE src-002] The benchmark does not cover multi-turn conversational voice interaction — all tasks are single-turn.
- [EVIDENCE src-002] Evaluation does not measure end-to-end latency, which is critical for interactive voice use cases.

### Inferred Gaps

- [HYPOTHESIS] No task in VoiceBench covers code-specific voice commands (naming variables, navigating functions, executing shell commands) — a major gap for developer-oriented voice tools.
- [HYPOTHESIS] The safety evaluation tests general jailbreaks but not domain-specific misuse patterns relevant to voice-driven OS control (e.g., voice injection attacks, command injection via dictation).

### Evaluation / Coverage Gaps

- [EVIDENCE src-002] All evaluated models are cloud-based or require GPU; offline CPU-inference models are not benchmarked. This leaves the quality of offline cascade systems (faster-whisper + quantised LLM) unknown against this benchmark.

## Applicability to Project

[EVIDENCE src-002] VoiceBench's task taxonomy (especially the instruction-following and coding task categories) provides a ready-made evaluation framework for YazSes once LLM intent routing is added. [HYPOTHESIS] YazSes could adopt a subset of VoiceBench tasks (instruction-following, coding) as its offline evaluation suite, adapting them for CPU-inference cascade architecture. This would give a principled quality baseline rather than informal "it works for me" testing.

[EVIDENCE src-002] The finding that instruction-following failure is a distinct failure mode (separate from transcription error) justifies YazSes adding a structured intent classification layer rather than relying on raw transcription. The disfluency filter already in YazSes addresses one failure category; an intent classifier would address the second. [HYPOTHESIS] A three-layer pipeline (transcribe → clean → classify intent) should outperform the current two-layer pipeline (transcribe → inject) across VoiceBench-style tasks.
