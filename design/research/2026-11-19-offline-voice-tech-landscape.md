## Permissive streaming models arrive as the OS absorbs dictation

The year's most consequential change for YazSes is that **the plain-dictation baseline is becoming a free, on-device OS feature, while the strongest new speech and language models are permissively licensed but GPU-first**. Windows fluid dictation now cleans up speech with on-device small language models, but only on Copilot+ PCs and in English locales ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/accessibility/windows/voice-access/history-of-voice-access-updates)). Apple's improved OS 27 dictation needs a device with at least 12 GB RAM ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/siri-ai-and-the-latest-in-apple-intelligence-the-macstories-overview/)). Ubuntu 26.10 ships Canonical's Myna as a dictation-only tech preview on GNOME/Wayland ([linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/ubuntu-26-10-steps-into-ai-with-local-speech-to-text/)). On the model side, Apache-2.0 and MIT checkpoints now top the open leaderboards: Granite Speech 4.1, Cohere Transcribe, Qwen3-ASR, Voxtral Realtime and Gemma 4. A new MIT runtime, transcribe.cpp, runs about twenty ASR families plus a diarizer and a VAD from GGUF files ([GitHub](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp)). That splits YazSes's opportunities in two. **Near-term wins** come from cheap moves that need no new engine: correcting stale comparison claims, a measured trial of pyannote community-1, a generic loopback endpoint for local LLM cleanup, local git-context hints, and MCP 2026-07-28 compatibility. **Medium-term bets** are adapters behind existing seams: Moonshine v2 streaming for dictation, transcribe.cpp as an accuracy tier for files and meetings, and a consumer for Apple's BCI HID switch protocol. YazSes's defensible position is no longer "offline dictation". It is **a verifiable no-cloud path** (the [ADR-019](../adr/adr-019-egress-inventory-and-escalation.md) egress inventory) combined with commands, accessibility inputs and local agent integration, on hardware and desktops the OS vendors skip.

## Leaderboard models favour files; small streaming models favour dictation

### The accuracy leaders are 1–4B parameters, BF16 and documented for GPU

The 2026 open-weight leaders share one profile: large decoders, permissive licences and GPU-only documentation. **IBM Granite Speech 4.1 2B** (released 2026-04-29, Apache-2.0, six languages) posts an Open ASR Leaderboard mean WER of **5.33**. Its `-plus` variant adds speaker-attributed ASR and word timestamps. The card lists llama.cpp GGUF and mlx-audio routes but no CPU speed ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b)). **Cohere Transcribe** (2B Conformer, Apache-2.0, 14 languages) scores **5.42**, but its own limitations section says it has **no timestamps and no diarization** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026)). That alone rules it out for the diarized path, because `recimport/align.py` needs time spans. **Qwen3-ASR** (Apache-2.0, 30 languages plus 22 Chinese dialects) scores 6.42 for its "0.6B" model, which the card lists at 0.9B parameters, and streams only under vLLM ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B); [arXiv:2601.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21337)). **Mistral Voxtral Realtime 4B** (Apache-2.0) streams natively with a configurable delay: FLEURS WER is 12.60 at 160 ms, **8.72 at the recommended 480 ms**, and 6.73 at 2.4 s. The card targets a GPU with 16 GB or more ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)). Mistral's docs already mark that checkpoint deprecated in favour of a "Realtime 3"; whether Realtime 3 has open weights was not verified ([Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/models/voxtral-mini-transcribe-realtime-26-02.md)). **Microsoft VibeVoice-ASR** (MIT) transcribes, diarizes and timestamps up to 60 minutes in one pass ([HF README](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-ASR/blob/main/README.md); [arXiv:2601.18184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18184)). At the far end, Salesforce's SOV-Transcribe-1 is a 26.6B Whisper-encoder/Gemma-MoE hybrid reported at about 2.3% WER **(unverified)** ([benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/models/sov-transcribe-1)); it is not laptop-class.

None of these pages gives a CPU real-time factor. On the YazSes reference laptop, where `small.en` already takes about five seconds, there is no evidence that any of them meets hold-to-talk latency. Their realistic role is an **opt-in accuracy tier for `yazses transcribe` and meeting files**, loaded as a Q4/Q5 GGUF rather than through a torch stack. Several of them also require Transformers v5 (Voxtral Realtime ≥5.2.0, VibeVoice-HF ≥5.3.0, Nemotron ≥5.13.0), which conflicts with the lean-install lesson in [docs/install-cost.md](../../docs/install-cost.md).

### Native streaming changes the premise of the 2026-11-03 streaming note

For dictation, the relevant arrivals are small models designed to stream. Each publishes an explicit latency/accuracy curve, and every curve is steep below about half a second and flat above one second. **NVIDIA Nemotron Speech Streaming En 0.6B** moves from 6.93 average WER at 1.12 s chunks to 8.43 at 0.08 s ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)). Its multilingual sibling, **Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming** (40 locales, OpenMDW-1.1), is reportedly the default on-device model in VS Code's new built-in dictation **(secondary)** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b); [YapToText](https://yaptotext.com/dictation-app-updates)). **Parakeet Realtime EOU 120M** emits an end-of-utterance token inline, with about 80–160 ms latency, and community ONNX INT8 exports exist ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet_realtime_eou_120m-v1); [soniqo ONNX INT8](https://huggingface.co/soniqo/Parakeet-EOU-120M-ONNX-INT8)). **Moonshine v2** uses sliding-window attention, so time-to-first-token stays bounded instead of growing with utterance length ([arXiv:2602.12241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12241)). It now ships as the MIT `moonshine-voice` package, which supersedes the non-streaming `useful-moonshine-onnx` checkpoints YazSes installs today ([GitHub](https://github.com/moonshine-ai/moonshine)). The strongest CPU evidence comes from Microsoft research. After benchmarking more than 50 configurations, it rebuilt Nemotron Streaming in ONNX Runtime with int4 k-quants, shrinking it from 2.47 GB to **0.67 GB** at an average streaming WER of **8.20%**, with 0.56 s algorithmic latency on CPU alone. The abstract does not say whether the weights are released ([arXiv:2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14493)).

This leaves the [streaming note](2026-11-03-streaming-endpointing-and-commit-safety.md)'s safety contract intact. Key release is still the end-of-turn signal for a held key, and partials still stay in the overlay under staged mode. What has changed is the design space. That note assumes streaming means LocalAgreement re-decoding of a growing Whisper buffer, so CPU cost grows with length. A cache-aware RNNT or bounded-attention encoder costs a fixed amount per chunk, and its partials are append-only within a chunk. That could remove some of the prefix-retraction cases the note catalogues, though only replay can confirm it. Separately, Parakeet's EOU token is a candidate model-native endpoint for the hands-free auto-stop mode, provided it satisfies [ADR-021](../adr/adr-021-invest-in-error-cost.md)'s rule that a guard must fire rarely. The licence is the binding constraint. **Moonshine v2 is the only small streaming family under MIT**; the Nemotron and Parakeet-EOU weights carry the NVIDIA Open Model License or OpenMDW-1.1, which the [model-manifest licence process](2026-11-06-model-manifest-licence-gaps.md) has not cleared.

### transcribe.cpp collapses the runtime grid into one GGUF adapter

The [2026-11-05 GGUF note](2026-11-05-small-quantized-asr-models-and-gguf-runtimes.md) compared whisper.cpp, sherpa-onnx and parakeet.cpp as separate candidates. Since then, **transcribe.cpp** (MIT, backed by Mozilla.ai as "llama.cpp for STT") has appeared. It runs Whisper, Moonshine and Moonshine Streaming, Parakeet, both Nemotron streaming models, Qwen3-ASR, Cohere, Granite 4/4.1, Voxtral Realtime and more. It also runs **Streaming Sortformer** diarization and **Silero VAD v6.2**. Backends are CPU, Metal, Vulkan, CUDA and ROCm; quantization presets run from F16 to Q4_K_M; bindings exist for Python, JS, Rust and Swift ([GitHub](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp); [Mozilla.ai](https://blog.mozilla.ai/announcing-transcribe-cpp/)). Its maintainer is the same person who maintains Handy, and maintenance is reportedly concentrated in him **(secondary)** ([runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/handy-creator-transcribe-cpp-local-speech-recognition)). The README shows no release tag, no PyPI wheel status and no CPU benchmark. Whether it is real for YazSes therefore depends on one unanswered question: **does the Python binding install as a wheel on Linux, macOS and Windows?** If not, YazSes would own a native build matrix. sherpa-onnx, already a YazSes extra for diarization, kept pace. Its 1.12–1.13 releases added Moonshine v2, Qwen3-ASR and Nemotron streaming ONNX re-exports, plus **Qualcomm QNN NPU** exports for Moonshine and Nemotron 3.5 ([releases](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases)). That gives the [accelerator note](2026-11-05-local-accelerator-backends-for-offline-asr.md)'s NPU matrix its first concrete Snapdragon speech path. whisper.cpp reached v1.9.x and still uses ggml `.bin` files, not GGUF **(secondary)** ([OpenBSD package](https://www.openbsdhandbook.com/packages/current/audio/whisper.cpp/)).

### Diarization and VAD have cheap, measurable upgrades

**pyannote community-1** (CC-BY-4.0, pyannote.audio 4.x) improves DER over 3.1 on most of the card's benchmarks: AMI-IHM 18.8→17.0, AMI-SDM 22.7→19.9, CALLHOME 28.5→26.7. It is worse on one (REPERE 7.9→8.9). It also adds an `exclusive_speaker_diarization` output, one speaker at a time, which suits word-level alignment ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1)). YazSes already requires pyannote.audio ≥4.0.7, but `src/yazses/recimport/pyannote_backend.py` still pins `PIPELINE_ID = "pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1"`, and nothing reads the exclusive output. A vendor-run cross-language benchmark found community-1 had the lowest DER on one slice but the worst on others **(secondary)** ([soniqo](https://soniqo.audio/benchmarks/diarization)), so an [ADR-033](../adr/adr-033-reproducible-asr-evaluation-gate.md) run has to decide the switch. NVIDIA's Streaming Sortformer v2.1 (4 speakers, NVIDIA Open Model License) and the newer Nemotron-3-Diarization (8 speakers, OpenMDW-1.1) would enable *live* speaker labels in meeting mode, the latter **(secondary)** ([Sortformer card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2.1); [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-drops-a-free-100m-parameter-model-that-identifies-up-to-eight-speakers-in-real-time/)). Both are gated on licence review. **Silero VAD v6** reportedly raises noise accuracy on ESC-50 from 0.61 to 0.87 **(secondary)** ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/what-is-silero-vad)). Loading its ONNX file directly, or through transcribe.cpp, would also sidestep the torch dependency of the `silero-vad` PyPI package that `meeting/silero_vad.py` already documents.

## Competitors converge on local cleanup, meetings and agent hooks

### Cloud leaders raise money and move beyond dictation

Venture money has validated the category and is pulling its leaders toward the cloud. **Wispr Flow raised $280M at a $2B valuation** in August 2026. It is expanding into a meeting Notetaker, its own speech model (Canto), Android and ring hardware, and TechCrunch says nothing about Linux or on-device processing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/wispr-raises-280m-at-2b-valuation-as-it-looks-beyond-dictation/)). Wispr reportedly moved every user onto Canto in September after complaints about quality **(secondary)** ([YapToText](https://yaptotext.com/dictation-app-updates)). That illustrates a durable advantage of pinned local models: a cloud user cannot keep the model they had. Wispr's optional Context Awareness "may collect limited, relevant content from the specific app in use (such as the text on the screen)" ([Wispr privacy policy](https://wisprflow.ai/privacy-policy)). Stronger claims that it took periodic screenshots come only from competitors **(unverified)** ([Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/is-wispr-flow-safe/)). The paid Mac tier has made **local LLM cleanup and bot-free meeting capture standard**. superwhisper 3.0 added a no-bot Meetings tab and made its local Whisper models free. VoiceInk added local "Refine". Spokenly lets MDM enforce local-only mode **(all secondary, from one competitor-run aggregator)** ([YapToText](https://yaptotext.com/dictation-app-updates)). Microsoft is winding down consumer Dragon. Dragon Anywhere stopped selling on 2026-07-01, and a partner reports that Dragon Professional Anywhere reaches end of sale on 2026-12-31 and end of life on 2027-12-31 **(secondary, unconfirmed by Microsoft)** ([Voicy](https://usevoicy.com/blog/dragon-professional-anywhere-end-of-life/)). That strands Windows professional and accessibility users who depend on command grammars.

### Open-source peers have caught up on breadth

YazSes can no longer claim to be the only free tool with "dictation, meetings and file transcription in one install". **Handy** grew from 29,533 to **39,322 stars** between August and November. Its v0.10.0 added **audio file transcription**, and its maintainer calls it "roughly feature complete as we move towards V1" ([Handy releases](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/releases)). **OpenWhispr** v1.10–1.12 added connectors that draft and send Gmail, Slack, Linear and GitHub actions behind approval cards. It also added an assistant window you can open from any app, a **Linux AT-SPI2 text monitor that learns from post-paste corrections**, and a Windows WASAPI process-loopback helper for meetings, and paid team tiers make it open core ([OpenWhispr releases](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/releases)). **FluidVoice** (macOS, GPL-3.0) shipped FluidMeet, local meetings with speaker labels. A beta lets **Claude Code and Codex read your meetings**, and the latest release adds Qwen3-ASR 1.7B, local cleanup models and opt-in screen context ([FluidVoice releases](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice/releases)). TalkType has been renamed **TuxTalk** and now offers Parakeet plus optional local AI cleanup ([GitHub](https://github.com/ronb1964/TuxTalk)). Linux gained Speed of Sound, a GNOME-first, portal-based app ([OMG! Ubuntu](https://omgubuntu.co.uk/2026/04/speed-of-sound-linux-voice-typing-app)). What YazSes still has that no single peer combines: **no cloud path at all**, enforced by test (OpenWhispr, VoiceInk and superwhisper all have cloud options); a voice-command grammar; accessibility inputs; and a cross-platform MCP server. FluidVoice's agent integration covers some of the MCP ground, but only on macOS.

### The OS baseline covers plain dictation on new hardware only

Each platform vendor gates its new dictation on hardware or desktop. Windows fluid dictation and Voice Access natural-language commands require Copilot+, the latter on Snapdragon ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/accessibility/windows/voice-access/history-of-voice-access-updates)). Apple's new model needs 12 GB of RAM, and its Foundation Models now come in local and Private Cloud Compute variants ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/siri-ai-and-the-latest-in-apple-intelligence-the-macstories-overview/)). Canonical's Myna is **explicitly not** a voice assistant, a command system or desktop control. It starts on GNOME/Wayland, with other desktops planned ([linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/ubuntu-26-10-steps-into-ai-with-local-speech-to-text/); [UbuntuHandbook](https://ubuntuhandbook.org/index.php/2026/10/ubuntu-announced-the-tech-preview-of-its-myna-speech-to-text-app/)). GNOME has only a Discourse proposal for desktop-wide offline dictation ([GNOME Discourse](https://discourse.gnome.org/t/proposal-desktop-wide-offline-speech-to-text-dictation-support/35858)). The space YazSes can defend is therefore specific: **KDE, X11 and non-Ubuntu Linux; older and non-NPU machines; cross-OS consistency; commands and hands-free control; LLM cleanup outside English** (superwhisper's S1-mini and Windows fluid dictation are both English-only); and regulated or air-gapped users who need verifiable egress rather than an MDM switch. Myna's narrow scope also suggests interoperating with it, for example over a shared portal or injection component, rather than competing on plain dictation.

## Small permissive LLMs make local cleanup and agent hooks practical

### Qwen3.5 Small and Gemma 4 replace the 2025 targets, under Apache-2.0

YazSes already ships opt-in local cleanup: a GGUF backend through `llama-cpp-python`, a loopback-only Ollama path, and length-ratio and critical-token guards in `postprocess/llm_cleanup.py`, all off by default. The models it would target changed in 2026. **Qwen3.5 Small** (0.8B/2B/4B/9B, March 2026) is Apache-2.0 **(secondary, several agreeing sources)** ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260303-qwen-3-5-small); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/de/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). **Gemma 4** (April 2026) is the first Gemma release under OSI-approved Apache 2.0 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/)). Its E2B and E4B variants have 2.3B and 4.5B effective parameters, 128K context, and audio input capped at 30 seconds ([model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)). Both licences remove the Gemma-terms and Llama-licence friction of a redistributable optional extra. Competitors aim higher: Handy's docs suggest "Qwen3 8B and larger" ([Handy docs](https://handy.computer/docs/post-processing)), which is unlikely to fit a CPU hold-to-talk budget. The task-specific evidence favours YazSes's current structure. The DRES benchmark found that reasoning-tuned models **over-delete** when removing disfluencies ([arXiv:2509.20321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20321)), which argues for disabling "thinking" mode and keeping the existing guards. The 2026 "Mind the Pause" paper tags disfluent tokens first and then rewrites with an LLM ([arXiv:2605.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12242)), the same deterministic-filter-then-LLM split YazSes uses. No source gave a CPU latency for these models on the cleanup task, so only a local benchmark settles which tier (0.8–2B for punctuation and "scratch that", 4B for tone rewrite) fits.

### A generic loopback endpoint beats pinning one binding

The embeddable CPU path is still llama.cpp. The newest `llama-cpp-python` found is 0.3.23, from May 2026 ([ecosyste.ms](https://packages.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/registries/pypi.org/packages/llama-cpp-python)), so it may not load Gemma 4 or Qwen3.5 GGUFs; that was not verified. A llama.cpp "v1.0" was reported in mid-November from conflicting sources **(unverified)** ([Neurohive](https://neurohive.io/en/news/llama-cpp-v1-stable-release-semver/)). Competitors avoid the binding problem with a loopback OpenAI-compatible server: Handy points at LM Studio on `localhost:1234/v1` and VoiceInk at Ollama on `localhost:11434` ([Handy docs](https://handy.computer/docs/post-processing); [VoiceInk docs](https://tryvoiceink.com/docs/local-models)). YazSes could generalise its existing loopback guard to any OpenAI-compatible loopback endpoint. That covers `llama-server`, LM Studio, Ollama and Foundry Local without a new outbound category, though the guard's registration under ADR-019 has to be confirmed. OpenVINO GenAI (`pip install openvino-genai`, CPU/GPU/NPU, release 2026.4) is a credible Intel extra ([OpenVINO release notes](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes)). Apple's official Python Foundation Models SDK (macOS 26+) costs nothing to download because the model ships with the OS, though Apple frames the SDK as an evaluation tool ([Apple docs](https://apple.github.io/python-apple-fm-sdk)). Phi Silica requires an MSIX-packaged app declaring `systemAIModels` and a Limited Access Feature unlock token from Microsoft ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/ai/apis/phi-silica-winui-tutorial)), so it is out of reach for an unpackaged Python daemon.

### Audio-LLMs merge recognition with instruction-following, and that is the attack surface

Gemma 4, Voxtral and Qwen3-ASR make single-step "transcribe and clean up" technically possible. The 2026 security literature argues against it for YazSes. **AudioHijack** shows imperceptible adversarial audio that makes large audio-language models follow hidden instructions. Secondary coverage reports 79–96% success and says in-context warnings "barely moved the needle" ([arXiv:2604.14604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14604); [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/ai-assistants-can-be-hijacked-and-manipulated-by-inaudible-sounds/)). **Sirens' Whisper** smuggles commands in near-ultrasonic audio ([arXiv:2603.13847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13847)). A Whisper-class recognizer followed by a guarded text LLM is not immune, but it does not hand the audio to an instruction follower. The structural defence that fits YazSes is to **keep ASR and cleanup as separate guarded stages and keep speech-to-action a closed grammar**. If a small model is ever used as a fallback intent classifier, it should be constrained with a GBNF grammar to an enum of existing command IDs, and pure code should re-validate every slot before `cmdsafety`. llama.cpp provides GBNF natively; llguidance is faster but needs a Rust toolchain ([llguidance](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance)). Constrained decoding guarantees the output's form, not its correctness. The bounded repeat count in commit 4a83e484 is the kind of check grammars complement but do not replace.

### MCP 2026-07-28 is breaking, and voice coding remains cloud-bound

MCP's `2026-07-28` revision is stateless. It retires `initialize` and sessions, carries version and capabilities in each request's `_meta`, replaces server-initiated elicitation and sampling with Multi Round-Trip Requests, deprecates Roots, Sampling and Logging for at least 12 months, and moves Tasks to an extension ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)). YazSes's `src/yazses/mcp/protocol.py` advertises `2025-06-18` and two older revisions. `ask_human` is a client-to-server tool call, so MRTR does not force a redesign. The open question is whether current clients still accept a `2025-06-18` stdio server, and that is a compatibility test, not a rewrite. Meanwhile, Anthropic's own Claude Code `/voice` "streams your recorded audio to Anthropic's servers… Audio is not processed locally", and does not work over SSH ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/voice-dictation)). Cursor added realtime two-way voice with agents **(secondary)** ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/cursor-antigravity-voice-agents/)). That leaves room for YazSes as the **offline, system-wide dictation layer feeding coding agents**, including over SSH, with the existing caveat that text can leave the machine once it reaches the agent. Two Claude Code details are worth copying locally. It adds the project name and git branch as recognition hints, which YazSes could derive on-device from the focused terminal's working directory through the existing `[context]` chokepoint. And it auto-submits only when the transcript has three or more words, a specific and checkable guard of the kind ADR-021 favours.

## Accessibility inputs converge on decode-on-device switch events

### Apple's BCI HID descriptor standardises the architecture YazSes already uses

The clearest accessibility signal of the year is architectural. Apple's **BCI HID reference** (iOS, iPadOS and visionOS 26.0+; **not macOS**) asks BCI makers to decode intent in firmware and present it as HID on usage page **0x60**. The device exposes 32 buttons (default 0 = select, 1 = next, 2 = previous, 3 = menu), a 1D/2D/3D pointer, an item-selection report and a signal-quality report. An output report tells the device the Switch Control scan state, and that report never reveals focus when the focused item is a keyboard key. The button action list includes **ToggleDictation** ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/brain-computer-interface-hid-reference-for-connecting-to-apple-platforms)). Synchron announced the first native integration in 2025 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/synchron-to-achieve-first-native-brain-computer-interface-integration-with-iphone-ipad-and-apple-vision-pro)). The design is YazSes's biosignal pipeline in standard form: decoded signal → discrete switch event → action. YazSes could write a **pure parser** for usage-page-0x60 reports, unit-tested with synthetic bytes and fed through hidraw, IOHID or Raw Input behind a platform Protocol. Any Apple-compatible BCI would then become a YazSes switch on exactly the desktops Apple leaves out: macOS, Linux and Windows. Whether Linux and Windows HID stacks expose that usage page to user space without quirks is untested. The signal-quality overlay and the keyboard-focus privacy rule are worth mirroring for EMG and ThinkGear regardless. Invasive BCIs remain out of product scope. Paradromics' first implant (June 2026) and Neuralink's speech trials have only company-reported results **(secondary)** ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/paradromics-real-time-speech-personal-devices)), consistent with the [feasibility report](state-of-the-art/2026-11-03-brain-interface-feasibility.md).

### The Meta Neural Band is the first mass-market wrist switch, reachable only unofficially

Meta's generic sEMG decoder works across users without calibration ([Nature 645, 702–711, via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v645y2025i8081d10.1038_s41586-025-09255-w.html)). Meta's own developer preview targets Ray-Ban Display apps on a phone, not desktops **(secondary)** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-opens-display-glasses-to-developers-with-two-sdk-paths/)). In September and October, community projects reverse-engineered the band's unencrypted BLE stream. **Kinesis** drives macOS with muscle gestures, "bypassing Meta's proprietary background services and cloud servers", and has a Linux Rust port ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Open-Source-Kinesis-reverse-engineers-Meta-s-Neural-Band-for-muscle-Mac-controls.1399361.0.html)). **RawMeta** (MIT) records raw 8-channel, 2048 Hz EMG at about 60 ms latency with no network after a one-time Meta-account enrolment. It warns that a firmware push can break it **(secondary)** ([Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/rawmeta-meta-neural-band-eight-channel-2048hz-emg-recorder/)). Discrete gesture events such as pinch or thumb swipe map cleanly onto YazSes's switch pipeline and are intentional motor events, not decoded thought. Any adapter must be off by default, lazily import its BLE library, and be documented as unofficial and fragile, including that the enrolment step happens outside YazSes.

### Eye tracking advanced in XR, not on the desktop

Open webcam gaze moved forward on two fronts. **UniGaze** (WACV 2026) ships ViT-B/L/H checkpoints that make a natural accuracy ceiling for YazSes's offline gaze replay, with ViT-B the only plausible CPU candidate ([WACV](https://wacv.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/oral/1185); [GitHub](https://github.com/ut-vision/unigaze)). A March 2026 MobileNet model claims generalisation competitive with UniGaze-H at under 1% of its parameters, but its paper is CC BY-NC-SA and the abstract gives no angular errors ([arXiv:2603.26945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26945)). Its augmentations for glasses, masks and lighting are still useful as a fixture design for the synthetic replay suite. Neither weight licence was checked. Platform eye input grew only in XR. visionOS gained Dwell eye selection, face gestures and **eye-driven power-wheelchair control**, and Apple says its eye tracking "doesn't require frequent recalibration" ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/apple-unveils-new-accessibility-features-and-updates-with-apple-intelligence/)). Galaxy XR added **single-eye tracking** ([Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-galaxy-xr-evolves-work-ai-era-new-enterprise-capabilities-everyday-features/)). For YazSes's calibration UX, that points toward monocular support and passive drift correction rather than repeated 9-point calibration. None of these platforms exports raw gaze to desktop software, so the XR bridge stays voice plus switch.

### Atypical speech gets a streaming benchmark, and fidelity becomes the differentiator

The **Speech Accessibility Project Challenge 2** (NeurIPS 2026) uses an 850-hour, 999-speaker dysarthric corpus under a data use agreement. It adds the **first streaming track for dysarthric ASR** and ships an open-source PyTorch latency-evaluation toolkit. In late 2024, offline ASR had 1.4% WER on typical speech against 17.8% on dysarthric speech; SAPC1 brought dysarthric WER down to **8.1%** ([NeurIPS SAPC2](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/competition/137691)). No winning checkpoint was found released. Adopting SAPC2's latency definitions would make the [dysfluency fidelity protocol](2026-11-03-dysfluency-fidelity-evaluation-protocol.md) comparable with the field. The corpus itself must never enter the repository or a third-party coding agent. Microsoft's Voice Access added recognition tuned for Parkinson's speech, and its fluid dictation removes fillers on device ([Windows blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsblog/2025/12/03/2025-a-year-in-recap-windows-accessibility/)). Filler removal is now a commodity. A **fidelity-preserving mode**, where removal is off or user-controlled, is the distinctive feature for dysfluent users. Lip-reading remains research-only: open Auto-AVSR rehosts report about 25–30% WER on clean speech (uploader claim) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/aaahmet/silent-lip-reader-model)), and an INTERSPEECH 2026 paper argues VSR leans on language priors rather than vision ([arXiv:2606.07435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07435)). If silent speech is pursued, it fits a constrained command vocabulary, not open dictation. On regulation, **EN 301 549 V4.1.1** (published 2026-09-02) moves to WCAG 2.2 AA but awaits citation in the Official Journal ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/09/european-accessibility-act-ict-standards-update)). A short conformance statement for the settings UI and tray would be a cheap trust signal for organisations deploying YazSes.

## Conclusion

The year's lesson is that **capability is no longer the scarce input; trust and reach are**. Accurate open ASR, small permissive LLMs and cross-user wrist decoders all exist under licences YazSes could ship. The cloud leaders are using their capital to move further from the machine. The OS vendors are fencing their offline features behind NPUs, 12 GB of RAM or a single desktop. That inverts the old risk to YazSes, which was being out-built. The new risk is being **out-described**: comparison pages that still claim differentiators peers have matched, while understating the ones nobody else has (enforced egress, commands, switch and BCI inputs, local agent hooks). The cheapest high-value move is therefore editorial, and the next is measurement rather than adoption: community-1, small cleanup models and Moonshine v2 each need one ADR-033 run before anything changes.

The second insight is architectural convergence. Apple's BCI HID descriptor, Meta's calibration-free gesture decoder, Microsoft's on-device SLM cleanup and the audio-injection literature all point to the same design: **decode on the device, emit discrete and checkable events, and keep free-form models away from the action path**. YazSes's existing split between a pure grammar and guarded backends is the shape the field is arriving at. The work ahead is to accept more inputs into that shape without loosening it.
